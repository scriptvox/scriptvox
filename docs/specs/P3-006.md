# P3-006: edit --reframe --track (the crop follows the subject)

Gap: a centre crop from 16:9 to 9:16 keeps 32 percent of the width and loses any subject that is not in the middle.
Reframe tools in the studied projects either crop the centre or need a detector service. ScriptVox uses the vision row
it already has (a free self-hosted Qwen3.8-27B on the owner's GPU service, as `scriptvox judge`), so no new pod or model is needed.

- `scriptvox edit <video> --reframe 9:16 --track [--subject "the camel"]`. `--track` without `--reframe` is a usage
  error.
- Shots: the picture cuts of `analyze` (4 fps scene test, threshold 0.25). Samples: about 2 frames a second in each
  shot, at least 1 a shot, at most 96 in all (then fewer a second), each a JPEG 512 px wide in
  `scriptvox-out/<name>.track/`. Requests of at most 12 frames ask for the subject's box per frame as `[x1, y1, x2, y2]` in 0 to 1000 units, or null. Measured on 3 Nov on a test
  frame with a known box: off by at most 3 units (0.3 percent).
- Path, per shot: a missing box repeats the last centre (the first box found, or the middle, at the shot start). A 3-point median drops one wrong box. A dead zone (4 percent of the
  frame) holds the crop still for small moves, so the frame does not wobble.
- FFmpeg: `scale` to cover the new frame, then `crop` whose `x` and `y` are piecewise-linear expressions of `t`
  through the smoothed centres, clipped to the frame. Each shot is held flat from its start to its first sample and
  from its last sample to 2 ms before the cut, so the crop jumps with the cut and never smooths across it. The crop runs before `--cut-silence`, so `t` is the input time.
- Envelope: `track.samples`, `track.shots`, `track.requests` and `track.x` (the path points). Exit 5 when the vision row fails (retry is safe).
- Known ceiling: 96 samples, so a video over 48 s gets fewer than 2 a second. A cut the scene test misses (two shots
  with the same colours, for example a mirrored copy) pans for up to one sample gap.
- Measured 3 Nov (second run): 24 frames in one request came back as 24 nulls (no error); 12 a request gave a box on
  every frame. So the batch is 12. A 15 s video of 3 shots (camel right, left, right): 31 samples, 3 requests, 19 s;
  the camel in the crop on 6 of 6 sheet frames, one of them 0.1 s after a cut. The centre crop of the 2-shot version:
  0 of 6.
- Measured 3 Nov: a synthetic red box moving left to right, tracked centres within 0.01 of the truth on 9 of 10
  samples (the first held by the edge median). A Wan 2.2 clip (camel at x = 0.75): the centre crop showed no camel
  on 5 of 5 frames, the tracked crop showed it on 5 of 5. 11 samples took one request of about 3 s.
- Tests: reply parsing with gaps, smoothing, the expression, sample counts (unit); a real run on a Wan 2.2 clip of a
  walking subject, checked by eye on a contact sheet against the centre crop.

## A subject bigger than the crop (G154)

A crop cannot follow a subject bigger than itself. `track` keeps the median box size from the replies. When the box is wider or taller than what the crop keeps, `edit` and `clips` give the warning `track_subject` with both percentages. The video is still made.

## A centre crop that cuts the face (G016, 6 Nov 2026)

`edit --reframe` and `clips` without `--track` had no finding when the crop cut the subject. Now one vision request reads 3 frames of the part kept and asks for the box of the face, or with no person the main subject (`--subject` wins). A box that reaches over 2 percent outside the middle gives the warning `centre_crop`, and the fix line names `--track`. The video is still made. No vision service or no usable reply gives no finding. A crop that keeps 95 percent or more makes no request.

Measured on the eval talk, 16:9 to 9:16, crop keeps 31 percent: warning on 3 of 3 frames, and the sheet shows half a face on all 6 frames. Same talk with the face moved to the middle: no warning, 2 of 2 runs. `clips talk.mp4`: warning on both clips. One talk only, so the false warning rate is not known. A hosted vision service bills this one request.
